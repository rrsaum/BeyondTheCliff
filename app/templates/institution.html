Federal financial responsibility score

Every private nonprofit and for-profit college that takes federal student aid files audited accounts with the U.S. Dept. of Education each year. The Department turns them into one number, from -1.0 to 3.0, and uses it to decide whether the school is sound enough to keep handling that aid. It is the closest thing to an official, annual read on a school's finances.

The financial record

Every private nonprofit college files a Form 990 with the IRS, in public, every year. It is the one place the same figures appear for every school on the same form, which makes it the only outside vantage on how a year actually went. What follows is what {{ inst.name | title }} filed{% if financials.gap_years %}, for the years it filed{% endif %}.

Read these together, not one at a time. Net assets say what the school is worth; earned revenue against expenses says whether a normal year pays for itself. The two can move in opposite directions for a long time. Institutional aid against tuition is the discount rate: the share of published tuition the school hands back as its own aid to fill the class. The 990 reports tuition before that aid is deducted and the aid separately as an expense, which is what makes the ratio readable at all.
