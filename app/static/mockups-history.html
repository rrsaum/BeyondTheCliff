How many colleges the US has had, 1949 to 2022: three mockups

All three use the same verified NCES series (Table 317.10), one hue. The question is how to show the rise and the fall and tie them to policy. GI Bill (1944) predates the series, so it is noted as context, not plotted. Pick one; it becomes the long-view chart on the page, above the closure map.

1 · the long line, with policy markers

The number of US colleges, 1949 to 2022

One line. Policy moments marked where they fall. The rise runs from the Title IV era through the for-profit boom to a 2012 peak of 4,726; the fall begins after gainful-employment scrutiny. The dashed lead-in is the pre-1974 branches-excluded era, a different count, so it is drawn as a lead-in and not joined solid.

Source: NCES Digest of Education Statistics 2023, Table 317.10 (degree-granting institutions, 50 states + DC). 1949 to 1969 exclude branch campuses; 1974 on include them, the seam is marked. Definition shifts to Title-IV degree-granting at 1996 (dashed). Latest published year is 2022-23.

Buys: the whole arc in one read, policy causes in place. Costs: a single total hides WHICH sector rose and fell; a reader cannot see that for-profits drove both.

2 · total, with the for-profit driver called out

What rose and fell was mostly for-profit

The same total line, plus the for-profit count (dashed, verified selected years). For-profits went 343 in 1990 to 1,451 in 2012 to 683 in 2022. That single component explains most of both the climb after 1972 and the drop after 2014, while public and nonprofit stayed roughly flat.

Source: NCES Digest of Education Statistics 2023, Table 317.10 (degree-granting institutions, 50 states + DC). 1949 to 1969 exclude branch campuses; 1974 on include them, the seam is marked. Definition shifts to Title-IV degree-granting at 1996 (dashed). Latest published year is 2022-23. For-profit line: verified NCES figures for 1990, 2000, 2010, 2012, 2020, 2022 only, drawn as a sparse series.

Buys: answers your actual question, what caused the rise and fall, on one chart. Costs: the for-profit line is selected years, not annual, so it is smoother than reality.

3 · five eras, five numbers

Five moments, and the policy behind each

Not a line: the count at five turning points, each tied to what moved it. Fastest read, for a reader who wants the story in five numbers, not a trend.
