Composite score chart: three mockups

Same data every time: Montreat College, Financial Responsibility Composite Score, award years 2006-07 to 2022-23 (U.S. Dept. of Education, eZ-Audit). Pick one, or tell me what to take from each.

What I need you to decide

  1. Which form. A reads the trend, B reads each year's verdict, C is a compromise.
  2. The y axis. All three cut it at 0.5 or 0. ED publishes the scale as -1.0 to 3.0. The old chart showed the full scale, which left 40% of the plot empty and squashed the data. Cutting it reads better but stops this school's chart being directly comparable to one that scored below zero. My recommendation: cut it, and let the shared thresholds do the comparison work.
  3. Colour by zone, or one colour? A, B and C all colour by federal zone (green / amber / red). It is legible but it makes a bad year look like a verdict. One accent colour throughout is more sober and more on-brand.

Mockup A

Line with zone bands

The trend is the subject: the line carries it, and the shaded bands say what territory it was in without you reading numbers. Threshold names sit in the right margin instead of on top of the data, which is what made the old one unreadable.

1.5 and above: responsible 1.0 to 1.4: added oversight Below 1.0: letter of credit

Strength: the shape of the story is instant, and the 2018 and 2020 dips read as events. Cost: a line implies continuity between what are actually discrete annual audits.

Mockup B

One column per award year

Treats each year as what it is: a separate audit with a separate verdict. No line implies anything between them. Every value is labelled, so the table underneath becomes optional.

Responsible Added oversight Letter of credit

Strength: the most honest about the data's grain, and the easiest to read a single year off. Cost: 17 bars is a lot of ink, and the trend is harder to see than in A.

Mockup C

Dot plot with a status strip

Least ink. Dots are the annual audits, the faint line is there only to guide the eye and never claims to be data, and the strip along the bottom answers "which years was this school in trouble" at a glance without reading the plot at all.

Responsible Added oversight Letter of credit

Strength: carries trend and per-year verdict together, and the strip is scannable. Cost: the faint line is a judgement call; some readers will still read it as interpolation.

All three: hover any mark for the year, score, and what it meant. The formula break is drawn and never joined across, since ED rewrote the ratios effective 1 July 2020 and the two sides are not the same measurement. Source: Financial Responsibility Composite Score, U.S. Dept. of Education (eZ-Audit).
