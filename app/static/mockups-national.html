The national picture for private nonprofit colleges: round 2

Round 1 started from the datasets and asked the reader to do the analysis. This round starts from the reader's question and answers it first. The closure history and the enrollment lines are now one section, because the finding is the tension between them.

  • Start from the reader's goal (Norman): a nonprofit president or trustee asks "is the ground moving under schools like mine, and which way?", not "show me closures by sector".
  • Lead with the answer (Krug's scan-first page; the inverted pyramid): the headline is the finding, the chart is the evidence, the table is the detail.
  • Compared to what (Tufte; the house principle): closures as a rate of the sector's own size, never a bare count, and every figure beside its history.
  • Reduce, then cohere (Ive): only the reader's sector, drawn with the components the site already has, so it reads as the same product.
  • Point past the cliff (brand rule): the closing line turns to what a school can do and links to the evidence, never a funeral watch.

Private nonprofit colleges enroll more students than ever. More of them are closing.

Fall 2023 enrollment in private nonprofit colleges was 4,182,357, the highest NCES has recorded. Over the same years the share of these colleges closing each year rose from 3.6 per 1,000 in the 2000s to 8.2 per 1,000 since 2020-21.

Our reading, not an NCES finding: the sector is not shrinking, it is sorting. The average private nonprofit college enrolled 2,546 students in 2022, against 1,507 in 1976. Averages do not show which schools gained; the per-school record in the tracker does. See the closure map
