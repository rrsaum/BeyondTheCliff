The financial record: three mockups

Same data every time: Montreat College, IRS Form 990, FY2013 to FY2025. Same visual language as the composite chart you picked. Each replaces BOTH charts in the current section. Pick one, or tell me what to take from each.

What changed in all three, and what I need you to decide

  1. "Total revenue" is gone. It was the third line on the old chart and it is just earned plus gifts, so it added a line without adding a fact. Say if you want it back.
  2. Which form. 1 shows the gap in dollars, 2 shows it as one ratio, 3 shows the whole composition. My recommendation is 2, because the finding is that the ratio barely moved in thirteen years, and only 2 shows that.
  3. Whether the FY2022-24 spike should dominate. Money charts (1 and 3) are ruled by it: government grants tripled the scale, so the first seven years get squashed. The ratio (2) is immune. That is a real trade, not a detail.

Mockup 1

The gap, in dollars

Two lines and the shaded distance between them. That distance is the subject: the part of every year that gifts and grants had to cover. The old chart drew this and never named it.

Strength: concrete, in dollars, and the gap is impossible to miss. Cost: the FY2022 jump compresses FY2013-19 into the bottom third, which is exactly where the school was in the most trouble.

Mockup 2

Share of expenses the school covered itself

One line: earned revenue divided by expenses. The green line at 100% is the year paying for itself. Everything below it is the gap, expressed as the number a CFO would use. This is the only version where the actual finding is visible: the line is flat.

Strength: immune to the scale jump, and it says the thing worth saying, that thirteen years of change left this ratio where it started. It also matches the composite chart's grammar exactly: a line, a threshold, zones. Cost: a ratio is one step removed from the money, and it hides that the school doubled in size.

Mockup 3

Where every dollar came from, against what was spent

Revenue stacked into what the school earned, what donors gave, and what government gave, with the expense line laid over it. One chart instead of two: the mix and the shortfall in the same frame.

Strength: the government-grant story tells itself, and it replaces both existing charts honestly. Cost: the densest of the three, stacked bands are hard to read a value off, and it inherits the same scale-jump problem as 1.

All three: hover any mark for the year and value. No return was filed for FY2020 or FY2021, so every line breaks there rather than joining across a year nobody reported. Source: IRS Form 990, FY2013 to FY2025, as filed.
