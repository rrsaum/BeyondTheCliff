# Beyond the Cliff: the book proposal (working document)

Status: v1 draft (2026-07-22). The plan for the trade book, and the skeleton of the proposal
that goes to agents. This is the internal working document; the agent-facing proposal is
generated from it. The book is the largest authority multiplier in `MASTER_PLAN.md` (W2) and
the flagship credential for the candidacy. Owner: R.R. Saum.

Sourcing rule, as everywhere: the failure cases and dollar figures below trace to the 2010
paper `why_dl_plans_fail` and its cited sources; the model findings trace to
`INSTITUTIONAL_HEALTH_METRICS.md`; the author platform figures trace to the handoff's
verified-figures table. Any figure carried into the actual proposal must be re-verified against
a primary source at the time, because the 2010 numbers are dated (see section 8).

## 1. The one-line pitch (the logline)

> **Colleges do not die of the enrollment cliff. They die of decisions they made years before
> the cliff arrived, and those decisions leave a signature you can read while there is still
> time to change course.**

The book is the argument that institutional failure is structural and legible, not tragic and
sudden, and that the same record which shows how schools fall shows how they climb.

## 2. The hook (why a reader who is not a college president cares)

Gladwell and Ariely sell to a general reader by making a specialist pattern feel like a law of
the world. The higher-ed frame has to open onto that wider door. Three candidate doors, to test:

1. **The autopsy door.** We learn how complex systems survive by studying the ones that failed:
   crash investigators, not the airlines, made flying safe. This book does that for colleges. The
   general-interest promise is a way of seeing failure in any institution as a readable signature
   rather than a mystery. This is the strongest door and it aligns with the brand's own
   crash-investigator framing (`POSITIONING_AND_BRAND_ARCHITECTURE.md` section 3).
2. **The foresight door.** Why do smart institutions, run by capable people, walk off a cliff
   everyone could see coming years out? The answer (structure beats intention; the org chart
   decides the fate) is a general pattern about why organizations cannot act on what they know.
3. **The prediction door.** A model that ranks which colleges will not survive, built from the
   public record, and what it reveals about the difference between a state and a fate. Riskier,
   because the brand's ethics forbid naming living schools; the book handles this at the sector
   and historical level, never as a living-school death list.

Recommended primary door: **the autopsy.** It carries the general reader, it is true to the
research, and it is the one that protects the presidency path.

## 3. The title

Working titles to test (the book keeps the brand name or gives it to the brand, either way):

- **Beyond the Cliff** (the brand name; clean, but abstract as a book title alone)
- **The Autopsy** / **Anatomy of a Closure** (the crash-investigator frame, concrete)
- **Dot-Com U** (from the 2010 source; evocative but dates the subject to one era)
- **The Signature of Failure** (the legibility thesis)
- **Why Colleges Fail** (the plain Ofsted-style frame the 2010 paper mirrors; strong, direct,
  and it pairs with a "Why Colleges Survive" back half)

Recommended: lead with a concrete title and the brand as subtitle, for example *Why Colleges
Fail: reading the signature of institutional survival* under the **Beyond the Cliff** banner.
Titling is genuinely the publisher's decision as much as the author's; carry two or three.

## 4. The argument (the spine)

The book runs the model from `MASTER_PLAN.md` section 3 as a narrative. Three movements:

1. **The fall is structural.** Institutions fail from where the risky venture sits relative to
   the core, not from the venture itself. The 2010 paper's axes generalize: internal versus
   external, integrated versus distributed. A bolt-on with its own P&L, cut off from governance
   and quality, dies; the same idea woven into the core survives. The cliff does not cause this;
   it exposes it.
2. **The signature is legible.** Failure leaves a trace in the public record years out: the
   enrollment slope, the discount that no longer fills the class, the reserves thinning, the
   composite score sliding toward the line. No single number is destiny (the value-trap lesson),
   but the combination ranks survival with real out-of-sample accuracy. The book shows the reader
   how to read the signature.
3. **The climb has a structure too.** The winners are not the biggest or the richest; they are
   the ones who read the signature early and made the moves while the moves were still cheap. The
   book ends on the climb, never on the fall, exactly as the brand requires.

## 5. Chapter architecture (draft)

Built from the 2010 taxonomy, the validated model, and the closure graveyard. Each chapter opens
on a scene (a named, historical failure or survival), then draws the general pattern, then hands
the reader the part of the model that chapter teaches. This is the Gladwell shape: story, then
the idea the story was carrying.

1. **The graveyard.** Open on the $263M-plus of named, dated failures the 2010 paper catalogs:
   Columbia's Fathom ($25M), NYU Online ($25M), the UK e-University ($113M, the biggest),
   AllLearn (Yale, Stanford, Oxford, $12M), Illinois' Global Campus ($7M). Capable institutions,
   serious money, all gone. The chapter's question: what did they have in common? (This is the
   sample chapter. See section 7.)
2. **The bolt-on.** The structural thesis: why the venture's *position* relative to the core
   predicts its fate. Internal versus external. The hero contrast: UMassOnline (integrated,
   roughly 40,000 students, profitable) against the external ventures that died.
3. **The instrument, not the medium.** The oldest lesson, from the 2010 paper's own sources: it
   is the method, not the delivery, that teaches; technology does not teach students, teachers
   do. Generalizes to: the shiny new thing is never the cause, of the failure or the success.
4. **The signature.** The model arrives as narrative: how the public record shows a school's
   trajectory years out, and why the combination of signals beats any single one. This is where
   the validated findings live, told as a way of seeing, not a regression table.
5. **The value trap.** Why the obvious signal (the deep discount, the falling selectivity) fools
   people, and why the give-away that no longer works is the real tell. Borrowed honestly from
   the investing analog in `INSTITUTIONAL_HEALTH_METRICS.md`.
6. **Irreversibility.** The book's original contribution: the difference between a bad state and
   a closed door. When can a school still turn, and when has it run out of moves? This is the
   chapter no competitor's viability score contains.
7. **The climb.** The survivors. Smaller, sharper, still vital. The moves the early readers made.
   The book ends here, on the way through, never on the body count.

Front matter (introduction) does the autopsy framing; the conclusion returns the general reader
to the wider door (how to read any institution's signature).

## 6. Comps (the "like X meets Y" a publisher needs)

- **Malcolm Gladwell** (*Outliers*, *The Tipping Point*): the general-reader pattern-as-law
  voice, the target register.
- **Dan Ariely** (*Predictably Irrational*): the specialist making counterintuitive structure
  feel like a law the reader lives under.
- **Clayton Christensen** (*The Innovator's Dilemma*, and *The Innovative University* for the
  higher-ed specific): the closest serious analog, why capable institutions fail structurally.
  The book should position as the field-data, post-cliff successor to Christensen's argument.
- **Robert Zemsky et al.** (*The College Stress Test*): the academic-market comp. Position as the
  trade-readable, narrative, wider-audience counterpart. Zemsky proves there is a market and an
  appetite; this book is the version a general reader finishes.

The one-line comp: *The Innovator's Dilemma for the enrollment cliff, told in the voice of
Outliers, by the analyst who called it in 2010.*

## 7. The sample chapter

Write one chapter to publishable quality: **"The graveyard"** (chapter 1). It is the right choice
because it is the most vivid, it needs the least of the model to land, it proves the author can
turn a citation list into scenes, and it carries the whole hook (capable institutions, real
money, a common signature). The 2010 paper already holds the cases and the dollar figures; the
work is turning them from a literature review into narrative, which is exactly the transformation
the whole book requires and the thing an agent is buying.

The sample chapter is a Wave 1 deliverable in `MASTER_PLAN.md` and gates the agent search.

## 8. What has to be rebuilt from the 2010 source

The prior art is the credential, but the numbers are dated (2007 to 2009) and predate the MOOC
era, the OPM collapse, and COVID-era online expansion. Before anything ships:

- **Re-verify every dollar figure** against a primary source at time of writing. The 2010 paper
  cites them; the book must stand on its own re-checked citations.
- **Refresh the market framing.** The $52B-by-2010 projection and the enrollment forecasts are
  historical artifacts now; use them as period detail (the hype of the moment), not current data.
- **Rebuild the cases as scenes.** The 2010 paper states the failures; the book has to dramatize
  them. This is writing, not research.
- **Answer the open question the author's own notes left unanswered:** *how do we measure success
  or failure?* The validated model now answers it in a way the 2010 paper could not. That is the
  book's forward motion: the question posed in 2010, answered with a working model in the 2020s.

## 9. The author platform (the section agents read first)

A trade nonfiction proposal lives or dies on platform and authority. Saum's is unusually strong
for a first-time trade author, and it is all handoff-verified. The proposal leads with:

- **Documented prior art.** The 2010 paper named the structural causes of online-learning failure
  before the sector proved them right. The author did not react to the cliff; he mapped its
  mechanism years out. This is the single strongest platform claim and it is provable.
- **Operator credibility.** Two P&L turnarounds at two scales: a distance-learning division grown
  from roughly 1,260 to 15,127 students over fifteen years (150,000-plus cumulative, $16M-plus in
  annual revenue, four consecutive U.S. News Top-10 online rankings), and a full division
  turnaround at Montreat (a +23.6-point enrollment spread against a declining market, with a 34
  percent cost reduction, in the same year). The author has run the institutions the book is
  about.
- **Sector standing.** SACSCOC peer evaluator; WCET Steering Committee. The author sits inside the
  bodies that govern the field.
- **The living platform.** The public tracker and the validated model are the proof the book's
  argument is real and the author owns the data. Most trade authors bring a thesis; this one
  brings a working instrument.

Present roughly 25 years of experience (per the handoff's display convention). Handle the
theological degrees neutrally, per the handoff. Reconcile the byline (Robert Saum vs R.R. Saum)
before querying; pick one and hold it across the book, the brand, and the CV.

## 10. Agenting and path to publish

- **Target agents** who represent idea-driven nonfiction and higher-ed or business authors (the
  Christensen, Zemsky, Gladwell neighborhoods). A short, researched list beats a mass query. The
  proposal from this document plus the sample chapter is the submission.
- **Sequence** (from `MASTER_PLAN.md`): proposal and sample chapter in Wave 1; agent search in
  Wave 2, alongside the flagship report (which doubles as proof the argument has legs); manuscript
  in Wave 3 if it sells; publication timed to the candidacy push in Wave 4.
- **The fallback is not failure.** If no agent bites after a serious effort, the hybrid-publish
  path (a sharp book under the existing Bound Less Living imprint, plus the flagship report doing
  the authority work) still delivers the durable-argument credential the candidacy needs. The
  authority requires the argument in permanent form, not a particular publisher's logo. Do not let
  the agent gate stall the report or the model, which stand on their own.
